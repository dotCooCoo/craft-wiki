This guide takes you through a first project in about 15 minutes. You make a square badge with a background, a circle, a star, a wavy line and a line of type, save it as a VectorCraft file and export it as a PNG and an SVG.

![The finished badge: a dark indigo square with an amber circle, a white star outlined in orange, the word Hello! in bold indigo letters and a light blue wavy line below the circle](/assets/uploads/vectorcraft/gs-badge.png)

*The badge you make in this guide, as exported to PNG.*

## 1. Get VectorCraft

Download and install VectorCraft for your computer. [Install and update](/apps/vectorcraft/install-update/) lists where to get it.

Open VectorCraft. With no document open, the Home screen shows **New file**, **Open**, any recent files and a row of presets under **Quickly start a new file**.

## 2. Tour the window

The window has the same layout on every platform. This screenshot shows the badge from this guide with the wavy line selected.

![The VectorCraft window with ten numbered markers: 1 on the menu bar, 2 on the Control bar, 3 on the document tab, 4 on the Tools panel, 5 on the artboard, 6 on the Contextual Task Bar, 7 on the column of panel icons, 8 on the panel dock showing the Properties panel, 9 on the hint line and 10 on the status bar](/assets/uploads/vectorcraft/gs-window-tour.png)

*The main areas of the window, numbered as in the following table.*

| # | Area | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Menu bar** | Holds the File, Edit, Object, Type, Select, Effect, View, Window and Help menus. The search button and the **Essentials** workspace menu are at its right end. |
| 2 | **Control bar** | Shows the fill, stroke, stroke weight, opacity and alignment of the selection. The Essentials workspace hides it; choose **Window › Control** to show it. |
| 3 | **Document tab** | Shows the document name, the zoom level, the color mode and the view. Each open document has its own tab. |
| 4 | **Tools panel** | Holds the drawing and editing tools. The Fill and Stroke boxes are near the bottom. |
| 5 | **Canvas and artboard** | The artboard is the white page that prints and exports. You can draw outside it on the gray canvas. |
| 6 | **Contextual Task Bar** | Offers the next likely actions for the selection, such as **Edit Path**, **Simplify** and **Duplicate**. Turn it off with **Window › Contextual Task Bar**. |
| 7 | **Panel icons** | Each icon opens a panel, such as Swatches, Brushes, Stroke or Character, beside the dock. |
| 8 | **Panel dock** | Shows the **Properties**, **Layers** and **Libraries** panels as tabs. Properties shows the settings of whatever is selected. |
| 9 | **Hint line** | Lists what the active tool does with a click, a drag and the modifier keys. |
| 10 | **Status bar** | Shows the zoom, the view rotation, the artboard navigator, the active tool and the pointer position. |

## 3. Create a document

1. Choose **File › New…** (Ctrl+N on Windows and Linux, Cmd+N on macOS), or press **New file** on the Home screen.
2. In the New Document window, choose the **Social** tab and then the **Social Square Post 1080×1080** card.
3. In the name field at the top of **Preset Details**, type `Badge`.
4. Press **Create**.

The new document opens with one 1080 × 1080 px artboard. Double-clicking a preset card also creates the document.

![The New Document window on the Print tab with the Letter card selected; Preset Details shows Untitled-1, 612 pt by 792 pt in Points, portrait orientation, 1 artboard, 0 pt bleed on every side, Transparent background contents, and Advanced Options set to CMYK Color, High (300 ppi) and Default](/assets/uploads/vectorcraft/gs-new-document.png)

*New Document the first time it opens. After you create a document, it opens on the Recent tab.*

The tabs across the top are **Recent**, **Saved**, **Mobile**, **Web**, **Print**, **Film & Video**, **Art & Illustration**, **Branding** and **Social**. Choosing a card fills in **Preset Details**:

| Option | Default (Print › Letter) | What it sets |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Untitled-1 | The document name shown on its tab. |
| **Width** and **Height** | 612 pt × 792 pt | The size of each artboard. |
| **Units** | Points | The units of the rulers and size fields. |
| **Orientation** | Portrait | Portrait or landscape. |
| **Artboards** | 1 | The number of artboards. |
| **Bleed** | 0 pt on each side | The area outside the artboard that prints. The link button keeps the four sides equal. |
| **Background Contents** | Transparent (Default) | Transparent or White. |
| **Color Mode** | CMYK Color | Under **Advanced Options**. The Mobile, Web, Film & Video, Art & Illustration and Social presets use RGB Color. |
| **Raster Effects** | High (300 ppi) | Under **Advanced Options**. The resolution of raster effects. The Mobile, Web, Film & Video and Social presets use Screen (72 ppi). |
| **Preview Mode** | Default | Under **Advanced Options**. Default, Pixel or Overprint. |

:::tip
The save button beside the name field saves the current settings as a preset on the **Saved** tab. **More Settings** shows the same options as a form, with the artboard layout, spacing and columns for several artboards.
:::

## 4. Draw shapes and a path

New objects get a white fill and a 1 pt black stroke until you change them. The hint line shows the keys each tool takes: with a shape tool, Shift+drag keeps the proportions, Alt+drag (Option+drag on macOS) draws from the center, and a click lets you enter an exact size.

1. Choose the **Rectangle Tool** (M) and drag from near the top-left corner of the artboard to near the bottom-right corner. This square becomes the background.
2. Choose the **Ellipse Tool** (L) and Shift+drag a circle in the middle of the square.
3. Press and hold the Rectangle tool's button to open its group, and choose the **Star Tool**. Drag outward from the center of the circle to draw a star.
4. Choose the **Pen Tool** (P) to draw a wave below the circle:
   1. Click once at the left to place a corner point.
   2. Click a little to the right and drag up to place a smooth point.
   3. Click further right and drag down to place another smooth point.
   4. Click at the right end, then press Enter to finish the path.

![Four shapes with a white fill and a thin black stroke on the 1080 by 1080 artboard: a large square, a circle inside it, a star inside the circle and a wavy pen path below the circle, selected and showing its anchor points; the hint line reads Click to add a corner point, Drag to add a smooth point, Click the first point to close, Enter to finish](/assets/uploads/vectorcraft/gs-shapes-and-path.png)

*The shapes and the pen path before any color is added.*

:::note
Clicking the first point of a path with the Pen tool closes it. [Draw and edit paths](/apps/vectorcraft/draw-and-edit-paths/) covers the Pen, Curvature and Pencil tools in detail.
:::

## 5. Color the shapes

Each object has a fill (the inside) and a stroke (the outline). The Fill and Stroke boxes near the bottom of the Tools panel show them, and the box in front receives the color you pick. Press X to bring the other box to the front.

1. Choose the **Selection Tool** (V) and click the square.
2. Choose **Window › Swatches** to open the Swatches panel.
3. With the Fill box in front, click a dark swatch such as Indigo.
4. Press X to bring the Stroke box to the front, then click the **[None]** swatch (the white tile with a red slash) to remove the outline.
5. Color the other objects the same way. In the example, the circle has an Amber fill and no stroke, and the star has a White fill and an Orange Red stroke.
6. Select the wave, set its fill to **[None]** and its stroke to Cyan, and type a stroke weight of `14` in the **Stroke** field of the Properties panel.

![The badge with color: an indigo square, an amber circle, a white star with an orange stroke and a light blue wave; the star is selected, the Swatches panel is open beside the dock with its Recent Colors row and swatch tiles, and the Contextual Task Bar shows Edit Path, Offset Path, Simplify and Duplicate](/assets/uploads/vectorcraft/gs-color.png)

*The Swatches panel applies a swatch to the Fill or Stroke box that is in front.*

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **X** | Brings the Fill or the Stroke box to the front. |
| **Shift+X** | Swaps the fill and stroke of the selection. |
| **D** | Gives the selection a white fill and a 1 pt black stroke. Type gets a black fill and no stroke. |

[Fill, stroke and color](/apps/vectorcraft/fill-stroke-and-color/) covers gradients, the Stroke panel and recoloring.

## 6. Add a line of type

1. Choose the **Type Tool** (T) and click inside the circle, below the star. VectorCraft places a line of placeholder text with all of it selected.
2. Type `Hello!` to replace the placeholder.
3. Press Esc. VectorCraft stops editing the text and switches to the Selection tool, with the type object still selected.
4. In the **Character** section of the Properties panel, choose a bold style and a size of `110 pt`, and set the fill to the same indigo as the background.
5. Drag the type so it sits centered under the star.

![The word Hello! in bold indigo letters inside the amber circle, selected; the Properties panel shows Transform, Character with Source Sans 3 Bold at 110 pt, Paragraph, Appearance and Align sections, and the Contextual Task Bar offers Create Outlines and Convert To Area Type](/assets/uploads/vectorcraft/gs-type.png)

*A selected type object shows its Character settings in the Properties panel.*

:::tip
Dragging with the Type tool, instead of clicking, draws a frame for area type that wraps inside it. [Add and style type](/apps/vectorcraft/add-and-style-type/) covers area type, type on a path and the Character and Paragraph panels.
:::

## 7. Arrange and group

Objects stack in the order you draw them, so the last object drawn is in front. The Layers panel lists them from front to back.

1. With the Selection tool, click the circle, then Shift+click the star and the type to add them to the selection.
2. Choose **Object › Group** (Ctrl+G on Windows and Linux, Cmd+G on macOS). The three objects now move and scale together.
3. Choose the **Layers** tab in the panel dock. **Layer 1** lists the group, the wave and the rectangle.

![The badge with the circle, star and type selected as one group; the Layers tab of the panel dock lists Layer 1 with Group, Path and Rectangle rows, each with a thumbnail and a visibility eye](/assets/uploads/vectorcraft/gs-group-layers.png)

*After Object › Group, the Layers panel shows one Group row for the three objects.*

To change the stacking order, select an object and choose a command from **Object › Arrange**:

| Command | Windows and Linux | macOS |
|---|---|---|
| **Bring to Front** | Ctrl+Shift+] | Cmd+Shift+] |
| **Bring Forward** | Ctrl+] | Cmd+] |
| **Send Backward** | Ctrl+[ | Cmd+[ |
| **Send to Back** | Ctrl+Shift+[ | Cmd+Shift+[ |

**Object › Ungroup** (Ctrl+Shift+G on Windows and Linux, Cmd+Shift+G on macOS) separates a group again. [Arrange, group and align](/apps/vectorcraft/arrange-group-and-align/) covers locking, hiding and the Align panel.

## 8. Save and export

### Save a VectorCraft file

1. Choose **File › Save** (Ctrl+S on Windows and Linux, Cmd+S on macOS).
2. The first time, a save dialog opens. Choose the VectorCraft file type, name the file `Badge` and save it.

The file gets the `.vectorcraft` extension, VectorCraft's native format, and the document tab shows its name. **File › Save As…** can also write the document as SVG or PDF; [Open and save files](/apps/vectorcraft/open-and-save-files/) lists the formats.

### Export a PNG

1. Choose **File › Export › Export As…**.
2. Set **Format** to PNG and turn on **Use Artboards** with **All** selected. Without Use Artboards, the export covers the bounds of the visible art instead of the artboard.
3. Press **Export…**, choose a folder and a file name, and save.
4. In **PNG Options**, check the settings and press **Export**.

![The Export As dialog with Format set to PNG, Use Artboards turned on with All selected, a note that each artboard is written to its own file named after the document and the artboard, and the Cancel and Export… buttons](/assets/uploads/vectorcraft/gs-export-as.png)

*Export As picks the format and the artboards before the file is named.*

| PNG option | Default | What it sets |
|---|---|---|
| **Resolution** | Screen (72 ppi) | The pixel size. At 72 ppi, one pixel covers one point, so the badge exports at 1080 × 1080 px. Medium (150 ppi), High (300 ppi) and Other are also offered. |
| **Background Color** | Transparent | Transparent, White, Black or Other. It starts as White when the document's Background Contents is White. |
| **Anti-aliasing** | Art Optimized | None, Art Optimized or Type Optimized. |
| **Interlaced** | Off | Writes an interlaced PNG. |

### Export an SVG

1. Choose **File › Export › Export As SVG…**.
2. **SVG Options** opens. Check the settings and press **OK**.
3. Choose a folder and a file name, and save.

The SVG file opens in web browsers and other drawing programs. [Export for screens and the web](/apps/vectorcraft/export-for-screens-and-the-web/) covers the SVG options and Export for Screens.

:::caution
**File › Close** (Ctrl+W on Windows and Linux, Cmd+W on macOS) asks whether to save a document with unsaved changes. Choose to save it if you want to keep your latest edits.
:::
